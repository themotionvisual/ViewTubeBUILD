# Diversity Audit

## Collection coverage

The initial 100-asset system spans 30 visual families and 12 registry categories. Current family counts across the full collection range from 2 to 7 entries. The largest family is `frame-system` (7), followed by `gradient-field` (6); no single family dominates the library.

The visual-family mix includes gradients, radial light, grids, tiles, orbital systems, line/wave fields, dot/halftone/checker/stripe patterns, frames, editorial layouts, split screens, technical/HUD systems, networks, particles, star fields, liquid/contour forms, isometric/perspective depth, tunnels, spirals, rings, light streaks, chromatic overlays, reveals and kinetic layouts.

## Motion-language coverage

Motion is not limited to rotation/pulse/drift. The renderer includes periodic:
- gradient focal movement
- scanning and dash offsets
- orbital trajectories
- contour deformation
- radial propagation
- wave phase interference
- modular tile phase changes
- tunnel depth progression
- editorial block translation
- perspective-grid travel
- dot-field radial modulation
- checker/stripe displacement
- radar scanning
- network node drift
- particle crosswind
- star twinkle/parallax
- liquid orbit/deformation
- isometric translation
- spiral rotation/dash travel
- ring echoes
- streak passes
- reveal apertures
- split-screen divider motion

## Review mechanism

Five `AssetSheet-NN` still compositions show 20 assets each using their actual production renderer. `AssetGallery` adds search/filter and live Remotion-timeline inspection.

## Remaining visual gate

A code-level diversity audit cannot substitute for looking at rendered frames. The collection is deliberately structured to avoid obvious metadata/family duplication, but final replacement of any visually weak or overly similar asset requires Studio/contact-sheet inspection in a runnable repository environment. No claim of pixel-level diversity certification is made before that pass.
